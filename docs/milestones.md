## Multiplayer integration constraints

- The user recalls that spending Spirit Strike power with one character depletes the meter for the whole party; this still needs a controlled gameplay check. Treat SSP as one host-authoritative party resource unless testing or reverse engineering disproves that model. Multiplayer activation must be serialized so simultaneous player requests cannot spend the same meter twice. Whether the value is physically stored once or mirrored between characters remains unconfirmed.
- Start camera work with a shared camera and a maximum player-separation rule. Per-player zoom limits can be widened later, but must not allow players to escape arena, transition, or scripted-event constraints.
- Before split-screen or independent player cameras, explicitly test Elco's scoped gun view and Ailish's ranged aiming view when another character is locally controlled. A camera attached to a character model may render that character as hidden, partial arms/weapon, or not at all for other viewports; each viewport therefore needs an explicit local-body visibility and camera-ownership policy.
- Native skill cinematics and Spirit Strikes must not seize every player's camera. Their eventual multiplayer policy may be local-only, replaced by a gameplay camera, or selectively authored per action, but this is a later camera-phase decision.
- Doorways and zone changes must not silently drag an exploring human player merely because the vanilla AI catch-up timer expired. First identify the transition trigger and recovery authority, then require all players at the boundary or an explicit group/host commit before the transition takes control.
- Cutscenes may place every party member even when gameplay positions differ. Trace entity identity across at least one scene before deciding whether to retain live actors, create scene-local stand-ins, hide absent players, or temporarily transfer control to the scene system.
