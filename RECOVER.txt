SudekiForge / model and world-authoring direction:
  - Prefer a separate sibling repository named sudeki-forge under a shared
    parent directory. SudekiMP should consume stable formats/libraries rather
    than becoming a combined reverse-engineering, editor, and multiplayer tree.
  - Maya is capable of professional polygon/NURBS/subdivision modeling,
    rigging, animation, layout, effects, and large scene/world authoring.
  - Maya does NOT solve Sudeki import/export by itself. The hard work is still
    decoding Sudeki model, skeleton, animation, material, collision, archive,
    navmesh, trigger, and world/scene formats, then writing converters.
  - Maya 2026 officially supports specific RHEL releases on Linux and its
    licensing has additional Linux library/display constraints. This is less
    convenient on a non-RHEL desktop.
  - Blender should be the primary first target: it is free/open source,
    cross-platform, scriptable with Python, contributor-friendly, and suitable
    for a custom Sudeki importer/exporter or add-on.
  - Maya can remain an optional authoring frontend for artists who already own
    and know it. Keep SudekiForge's intermediate representation independent of
    either DCC so Blender, Maya, and future tools can share converters.
  - Neither Blender nor Maya is automatically a complete Sudeki world editor.
    Long-term world building likely needs a Blender add-on or dedicated
    SudekiForge scene editor for collision, spawn points, triggers, doors,
    scripts, nav data, cameras, and archive packaging.
